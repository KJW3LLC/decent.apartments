**How to Think About Roommates Before Signing a Lease** 🚨
====================================================================

Finding a place to call home is exciting, but picking the right roommate can make—or break—the experience. I’ve seen friends thrive in shared spaces and others dread coming home because the vibe was off. Let’s walk through a practical, friendly checklist so you can move in with confidence (and maybe even a new bestie).

## Quick Context
This guide is for anyone hunting an apartment on Decent Apartments who’s considering sharing a lease—whether you’re a first‑time renter, a student, or someone looking to cut costs. It matters most before you sign anything, because once the lease is locked in, changing roommates gets messy fast.

## Start with Self‑Reflection
Before you even look at potential roommates, get clear on what you need from a living situation.

> **💡 Pro Tip:** Write down three non‑negotiables (e.g., quiet after 10 pm, no pets, split chores evenly) and three flexible preferences. This keeps you from compromising on what truly matters.

Ask yourself:
- Do I need a quiet environment for work or study?
- Am I okay with frequent guests or prefer a low‑key household?
- How do I feel about sharing food, cleaning supplies, or communal spaces?

Knowing your own boundaries makes it easier to spot mismatches early.

## Have the Money Talk Early
Money is the most common source of tension, so bring it up before you fall in love with a unit.

> **⚠️ Watch Out:** Assuming “we’ll figure it out later” often leads to resentment. Get concrete numbers on the table now.

Discuss:
- Exact rent split (will it be 50/50, based on room size, or another formula?)
- Utilities, internet, and any shared subscriptions (streaming, gym)
- How you’ll handle groceries or household supplies (shared pot, separate bills, or a hybrid?)
- What happens if someone’s income changes unexpectedly (e.g., job loss, reduced hours)

Putting these details in a simple shared document—or even a group chat note—creates transparency and reduces future awkwardness.

## Set Boundaries & Expectations
Living together means sharing space, so clarify daily habits early on.

> **🎯 Key Insight:** Small habits (like leaving dishes in the sink or blasting music) become big frustrations if never addressed.

Consider drafting a loose “roommate agreement” that covers:
- Quiet hours and guest policies
- Cleaning schedule (who does what, and how often)
- Rules about smoking, vaping, or candles
- Procedure for handling conflicts (e.g., a weekly check‑in or a neutral mediator)

Even if you don’t make it formal, verbalizing these points shows respect and sets a tone of cooperation.

## Test Compatibility Before Lease
A quick coffee chat isn’t enough—spend some time together in a low‑pressure setting.

- **Hang out in a neutral spot:** Grab a bite or do a short activity to see how you communicate.
- **Do a mini “trial run”:** If possible, stay over at each other’s place for a night. Notice how you handle shared bathrooms, noise levels, and personal space.
- **Ask about lifestyle:** Work schedules, sleep habits, social energy, and even pet preferences can reveal compatibility (or red flags).

Trust your gut—if something feels off now, it’s likely to amplify under the pressure of a shared lease.

## Positive Signs and Negative Signs
Knowing what to look for helps you decide quickly.

### Positive Signs 🌟
- Open, honest conversation about finances and habits
- Similar cleanliness standards or willingness to compromise
- Matching schedules (e.g., both early risers or night owls)
- Enthusiasm about creating a shared home, not just splitting rent
- Respect for each other’s personal space and belongings

### Negative Signs 🚩
- Vague or evasive answers about money or bills
- History of frequent roommate turnover (ask why previous arrangements ended)
- Disrespect for boundaries during early conversations (e.g., showing up uninvited)
- Drastically different lifestyles with no willingness to meet halfway
- Pressure to sign quickly without discussing details

If you notice more red flags than green, it’s worth pausing and reconsidering.

## What to Check Before You Decide
Before you put pen to paper (or click “sign”), run through this practical checklist.

- **Verify each person’s income stability:** Ask for recent pay stubs or an offer letter if you feel comfortable; otherwise, discuss openly how rent will be covered if circumstances shift.
- **Confirm lease details:** Ensure everyone’s name will be on the lease (or a legally sound sublet agreement) so liability is shared.
- **Discuss exit strategy:** What happens if someone needs to move out early? Understand the process for finding a replacement and any fees involved.
- **Review the apartment together:** Walk through the unit, note any existing damage, and take photos—this protects everyone’s security deposit later.
- **Set up a shared expenses app:** Tools like Splitwise or Venmo make tracking bills painless and transparent.

Taking these steps now saves headaches (and possible legal tangles) down the road.

## Key Takeaways
- **Know your own needs** before you start looking for a roommate.
- **Talk money early**—be specific about rent, utilities, and shared costs.
- **Set clear expectations** around cleaning, guests, and quiet hours.
- **Spend time together** in a low‑stress setting to gauge compatibility.
- **Watch for green and red flags**—honesty and flexibility are good signs; vagueness and disrespect are warnings.
- **Verify logistics** (lease names, exit plan, damage docs) before signing anything.

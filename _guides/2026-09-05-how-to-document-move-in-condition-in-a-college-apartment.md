**How to Document Move-In Condition in a College Apartment** 🏠  
====================================================================

Moving into a college apartment can feel like a whirlwind of boxes, roommate introductions, and that first sip of coffee in your new kitchen. Taking a little time to document the place *before* you unpack can save you headaches—and maybe even your security deposit—later on. Trust me, a few photos and a quick checklist now are worth their weight in ramen noodles when move‑out day rolls around.

## Quick Context  
This guide is for students (or anyone) signing a lease for a college‑town apartment, whether you’re a freshman nervous about your first place or a senior looking to avoid deposit disputes. The steps work best right after you get the keys and before you start arranging furniture, but you can still do a solid walk‑through even if you’ve already moved in a bit.

## Gather Your Tools  
> **💡 Pro Tip:** Grab your smartphone, a notebook (or notes app), and a flashlight—natural light can hide scuffs in corners.

You don’t need fancy gear. A phone with a decent camera, a pen, and a simple checklist (more on that below) are all you really need. If you have a roommate, enlist them; two sets of eyes catch more than one.

## Do a Walkthrough Checklist  
Create a quick list of the usual suspects: walls, floors, windows, doors, appliances, fixtures, and any furniture that came with the place. Walk through each room slowly, ticking off items as you go.  

> **⚠️ Watch Out:** Don’t forget to check inside closets, under sinks, and behind doors—damage often hides where you least expect it.

If you spot something, note it immediately: “Living room – small scratch near baseboard, about 2 inches long.” Being specific makes it harder for anyone to claim the issue was pre‑existing later.

## Take Photos & Videos  
> **🎯 Key Insight:** A timestamped photo is worth a thousand words—especially when memories fade.

Snap clear pictures of each wall, ceiling, and floor. Open cabinets and drawers to show their interior condition. Record a short video walk‑through (15‑30 seconds per room) while narrating what you’re seeing; the audio timestamp adds another layer of proof. Most phones automatically add date/time stamps, but you can also hold up a piece of paper with the date in the shot for extra clarity.

## Create a Written Record  
After your visual sweep, compile your notes into a simple document:  

- **Header:** Property address, move‑in date, your name(s)  
- **Body:** Bullet‑point list of each issue you found, referencing the photo/video file names (e.g., “Photo_001.jpg – kitchen cabinet hinge loose”)  
- **Footer:** Your signature and the date you completed the checklist  

Save this as a PDF and email it to yourself (and your landlord/property manager) right away. Keeping a copy in your cloud storage means you’ll never lose it.

## Share with Landlord/Roommates  
> **💡 Pro Tip:** Send a friendly email saying, “Hey, I just wanted to share my move‑in condition notes so we’re all on the same page—let me know if you see anything I missed!”

Transparency builds trust. If your landlord responds with their own notes, great—you now have a mutual record. If they don’t reply, you still have your documentation protected.

## Positive Signs and Negative Signs  
Understanding what to look for helps you separate normal wear‑and‑tear from actual damage.

### Positive Signs (what’s usually okay)  
- Minor scuffs on walls that can be wiped clean  
- Light wear on carpet in high‑traffic areas (no stains or tears)  
- Slightly loose cabinet hinges that tighten with a screwdriver  
- Faded paint from sunlight (not peeling or cracking)

### Negative Signs (flag these)  
- Large holes, cracks, or water stains on walls/ceilings  
- Broken windows, missing screens, or locks that don’t latch  
- Appliances that don’t turn on, make strange noises, or show rust  
- Persistent odors (mildew, smoke) that linger after cleaning  
- Furniture provided by the landlord that is broken or missing parts  

If you see any of the negative signs, note them immediately and consider asking for repairs before you sign off on the move‑in condition.

## What to Check Before You Decide  
Before you even commit to a lease, a quick pre‑move‑in scan can save you future trouble:

- **Test all lights and switches** – flickering could indicate wiring issues.  
- **Run water in sinks and showers** – check for drainage speed and leaks.  
- **Flush toilets** – ensure they refill properly and don’t run constantly.  
- **Open and close every door and window** – look for sticking, gaps, or broken locks.  
- **Smell for mustiness** – especially in basements or closets; could hint at mold.  
- **Ask about pest history** – a quick question can reveal if routine treatments are needed.  

If anything feels off, bring it up with the landlord *before* you sign. It’s easier to negotiate repairs or a rent adjustment now than to fight over a deposit later.

## Key Takeaways  
- **Document early:** Do your walkthrough, photos, and notes before unpacking.  
- **Be specific:** Write down exact locations and descriptions of any issues.  
- **Use timestamps:** Photos/videos with dates (or a dated note in the shot) strengthen your record.  
- **Share promptly:** Email your documentation to yourself and the landlord/roommate.  
- **Know the difference:** Normal wear‑and‑tear is fine; damage needs to be recorded and possibly repaired.  
- **Stay friendly:** A cooperative tone makes the process smoother for everyone.
